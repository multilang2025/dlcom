UK inheritance tax in Spain: key points

  • Since 6 April 2025, UK inheritance tax reaches your worldwide assets while you are a long-term UK resident, meaning UK resident for at least 10 of the previous 20 tax years (Inheritance Tax Act 1984, section 6A).
  • After you leave the UK, that status lasts 3 tax years if you were UK resident for 13 or fewer of the previous 20 tax years, rising by one year for each extra year of residence to a maximum of 10 tax years (HMRC manual IHTM47020).
  • Property situated in the UK generally stays within UK inheritance tax whatever your residence, because section 6(1) excludes only property outside the UK, and only for owners who are not long-term UK residents.
  • There is no UK-Spain inheritance tax treaty: HMRC's list of inheritance tax conventions does not name Spain.
  • Spanish succession tax is charged on each heir, not on the estate: an heir resident in Spain is taxed on everything inherited, and a non-resident heir on assets located in Spain (Ley 29/1987, articles 5 to 7).
  • Where Valencian rules apply, inheritances received by spouses, children and other direct relatives (groups I and II) get a 99% bonus on the Spanish tax due, for deaths on or after 28 May 2023, on assets declared in the return (Ley 13/1997, article 12 bis).
  • The Spanish inheritance tax return (Modelo 650) is due within six months of the death, and an extension must be requested within the first five months (Real Decreto 1629/1991, articles 67 and 68).

Two inheritance tax systems and no treaty between them

UK inheritance tax in Spain is a live question for British people who move to Spain, keep UK assets or have family on both sides of the border. Since 6 April 2025 the UK asks how many of the last 20 tax years you were UK resident, not where you are domiciled, to decide whether your overseas assets are taxable.

Moving to Spain does not end that exposure on arrival. For 3 to 10 tax years your worldwide estate can stay inside the UK net, while Spain taxes your heirs under its own rules.

This post covers Spanish law and the Valencian angle, and explains the UK rules only as far as they affect Spanish planning, so please coordinate any UK advice and filing with your UK adviser. The law is stated as at 6 October 2026.

The UK long-term residence test

The test sits in section 6A of the Inheritance Tax Act 1984, inserted by section 44 of the Finance Act 2025 from 6 April 2025. It replaced the domicile and deemed domicile rules for deaths and transfers on or after that date, while domicile still matters for earlier events.

“For the purposes of this Act, an individual is a ‘long-term UK resident’ at all times in a tax year if they were UK resident for at least 10 of the previous 20 tax years.”

Section 6A(1), Inheritance Tax Act 1984 on legislation.gov.uk. UK residence in a given year is decided under the statutory residence test, which HMRC explains in its residence guidance.

HMRC's guidance says that, while you are a long-term UK resident, Inheritance Tax is charged on overseas assets you own outright when you transfer them or die, and on overseas assets in a trust you have set up or added to.

The standard rate is 40%, charged only on the part of the estate above the threshold, which gov.uk gives as £325,000.

Leaving the UK: the 3 to 10 year tail

Leaving the UK does not take you out of the net straight away. You stop being a long-term UK resident only if you were non-UK resident for 10 consecutive tax years in the 19 before the current year, or “for at least the required number of consecutive tax years ending with the tax year before the current tax year”.

To find the required number, take the 20 tax years ending with your last UK-resident year and count how many were UK-resident years. The table follows section 6A(3), and HMRC's manual at IHTM47020 presents the same figures.

Someone UK resident for 15 of those 20 years stays within the rules for 5 tax years after leaving, and someone resident for all 20 for 10. Both routes out count consecutive years, so a return to UK residence interrupts the count.

The HMRC guidance also sets transitional rules for people who left in the 2025 to 2026 tax year, and section 6A(6) points to rules for young persons and elections. Ask your UK adviser to confirm the year in which your own tail ends.

UK property that stays inside UK inheritance tax

“Property situated outside the United Kingdom is excluded property if the person beneficially entitled to it is an individual who is not a long-term UK resident.”

Section 6(1), Inheritance Tax Act 1984 on legislation.gov.uk.

The exclusion speaks only of property outside the UK. A UK home, UK land or other UK-situated assets are not covered by it, and they remain in the UK charge whichever country you live in. Other UK rules can exempt particular assets, which your UK adviser can confirm.

How Spain taxes inheritances: the heir pays

Spanish succession and gift tax (Impuesto sobre Sucesiones y Donaciones, ISD) is governed by Ley 29/1987. The taxable event is the acquisition of assets by inheritance or gift, and the taxpayer is the heir or donee (articles 3 and 5), not the estate.

Under article 6, an heir habitually resident in Spain is taxed on what they inherit whatever the location of the assets, and residence follows the personal income tax rules. A British heir living in Spain who inherits a UK house is therefore within Spanish tax on it, while UK inheritance tax may also apply to the estate it came from.

Under article 7, a non-resident heir is taxed on assets located in Spain and rights exercisable there. The state scale in article 21 runs from 7.65% to 34%, before the reductions and bonuses that the Communities add.

Which Community's rules apply to a non-resident

The second additional provision of the Act, as amended by Ley 11/2021 of 9 July, lets non-resident taxpayers apply a Community's own rules. The consolidated Act sets out the main cases:

  • If the deceased was not resident in Spain, the heirs apply the rules of the Community where the greatest value of the Spanish assets lies. If there are no Spanish assets, each heir applies the rules of the Community where they live.
  • If the deceased was resident in a Community, non-resident heirs apply its rules.
  • For gifts of Spanish real estate, a non-resident donee applies the rules of the Community where the property is located.

The provision adapts the tax to the EU Court of Justice judgment of 3 September 2014 in Case C-127/12. Its earlier text, amended by Ley 26/2014, covered only EU and EEA residents, and Ley 11/2021 removed that limit.

Valencian Community bonuses and reductions

The Valencian rules are in Ley 13/1997. The Generalitat's tax agency announced the 99% bonus in a notice and lists current reliefs on its 2026 tax benefits page.

Under article 12 bis.2, that means assets included in full in a return filed within the voluntary period, or later but before a prior requirement from the tax office. Step-children fall in group III, and the 25% bonus is limited to blood relatives.

No UK-Spain treaty: relief from double taxation

HMRC's double taxation relief page lists conventions with Ireland, South Africa, the USA, the Netherlands, Sweden and Switzerland, and older arrangements with France, Italy, India and Pakistan. Spain is not on it. Our UK Spain double taxation guide covers the separate income tax treaty.

Where there is no convention, the same page says you may get unilateral relief. HMRC gives credit against UK Inheritance Tax for the tax charged by another country on assets sited in that country, limited to the UK tax on that asset.

On the Spanish side, article 23 of Ley 29/1987 gives an heir taxed as a Spanish resident a deduction for similar tax paid abroad, capped at the lesser of two amounts the article defines.

The sources do not say how these two credits work together for a given family. UK tax is generally charged by reference to the estate and Spanish tax on the heir, so whether the UK charge is a similar tax for a Spanish heir is a point to confirm with us and your UK adviser. HMRC's credit is for the tax the other country charges, so a Valencian bonus that lowers the Spanish tax also lowers the credit.

Succession law and tax are separate questions

Regulation (EU) No 650/2012 applies in Spain and decides which law governs a succession, not which tax applies. Under article 20 the law it specifies applies “whether or not it is the law of a Member State”.

“Unless otherwise provided for in this Regulation, the law applicable to the succession as a whole shall be the law of the State in which the deceased had his habitual residence at the time of death.”

Article 21(1), Regulation (EU) No 650/2012 on EUR-Lex.

After a move to Spain, the default law is therefore Spanish law, unless the exception in article 21(2) for a manifestly closer connection to another State applies. Article 22(1) lets a person choose “the law of the State whose nationality he possesses at the time of making the choice or at the time of death”, and article 22(2) says the choice is made in the will itself, expressly or as shown by its terms.

The choice decides who inherits, not the tax, because article 1(1) says the Regulation “shall not apply to revenue, customs or administrative matters”. Deadlines and reporting in Spain

  • Modelo 650 (inheritance): due within six months of the death under article 67 of Real Decreto 1629/1991. An extension of the same length can be requested within the first five months (article 68), and interest runs on the days after the first six months.
  • Where it is filed: the Valencian tax agency handles a deceased who lived in the Community for the longest part of the previous five years, and says filing is with the AEAT if the taxpayer or the deceased is not resident in Spain.
  • Modelo 720: Spanish residents report UK assets above €50,000 in a category between 1 January and 31 March of the following year, as the AEAT deadlines page explains. See our Modelo 720 article.

UK reporting and payment belong to your UK adviser and the personal representatives. On the Spanish side, start the file as soon as the death certificate is available, because the six months run from the death.

Planning checklist before and after you move

Before you move

  1. Count the tax years you were UK resident in the last 20 and use the tail table to estimate when you leave the UK net.
  2. Ask your UK adviser to estimate UK inheritance tax on your worldwide estate for the tail years, including trusts and pensions. A government policy paper of 26 November 2025 says most unused pension funds will fall within the estate from 6 April 2027, so check its status.
  3. Review your UK will and decide whether you need a Spanish will for Spanish assets, with an article 22 choice of law and no clash between the two wills.
  4. Review lifetime gifts. HMRC treats most gifts as exempt if you survive seven years, but Spain taxes a donee resident in Spain when the gift is made, subject to any regional bonus.

After you move

  1. Record your UK residence status each tax year and keep the evidence.
  2. Update wills and beneficiary designations for your new residence and chosen law.
  3. Report UK assets on Modelo 720 above the threshold, and remember that heirs living in Spain are taxed on their worldwide inheritance.

Frequently asked questions about UK inheritance tax in Spain

Does moving to Spain end UK inheritance tax on my estate?

Not on its own. The long-term UK resident status continues for 3 to 10 tax years after you leave, and UK-situated property generally stays within the charge whatever your residence.

Is there an inheritance tax treaty between the UK and Spain?

No. HMRC's list of inheritance tax conventions does not name Spain. HMRC allows unilateral relief for tax charged by the other country on assets sited there.

Will my children in Spain pay Spanish tax on what they inherit from me in the UK?

If they are habitually resident in Spain, article 6 of Ley 29/1987 taxes them on what they inherit wherever it is located. UK inheritance tax may also apply to the estate. Contact us to confirm how the two interact for your family.

Does the Valencian 99% bonus apply to British heirs?

The bonus is written for relatives in groups I and II, and non-resident heirs may apply a Community's own rules. Whether it applies to a given British heir depends on where the deceased lived, where the assets are and what is declared. Contact us to confirm your case.

When is the Spanish inheritance tax return due?

Within six months of the date of death, with an extension of six months available if it is requested within the first five months.

Does choosing English law for my Spanish will change the tax?

No. Regulation 650/2012 decides which law governs the succession, and article 1(1) excludes revenue matters.

Do I need a will in Spain and in the UK?

A Spanish will for Spanish assets is usually easier for heirs to use in Spain, as our article on writing a Spanish will explains. Draft the wills so that neither revokes the other, with your UK adviser reviewing the UK one.
