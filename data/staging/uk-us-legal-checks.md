## 1. UK inheritance tax: long-term residence test. Verdict: CONFIRMED (UK side)

Verified facts for the drafter:
- From 6 April 2025 an individual "is a 'long-term UK resident' at all times in a tax year if they were UK
  resident for at least 10 of the previous 20 tax years" (IHTA 1984 s.6A(1), inserted by Finance Act 2025
  s.44(3)(4), effective 6.4.2025).
- The status ends if the person was non-UK resident for any 10 consecutive tax years in the 19 before, or for the
  "required number" of consecutive years ending with the previous tax year. Required number by years of UK
  residence in the 20 years to the last UK-resident year: 13 or fewer = 3; 14 = 4; 15 = 5; 16 = 6; 17 = 7; 18 = 8;
  19 = 9; 20 = 10 (s.6A(2)-(3)). So the "tail" is 3 to 10 tax years.
- HMRC guidance: if you are a long-term UK resident, "your non-UK (overseas) assets may be subject to Inheritance
  Tax if you make a transfer of assets or die". Domicile and deemed domicile were replaced for deaths and
  transfers from 6 April 2025 (IHTM47001: domicile still counts for earlier events, for some trusts, and where a
  double tax convention uses domicile).
- Use the statute's wording for the test. The gov.uk guidance bullet ("previous 10 years consecutive years" or "a
  total of 10 years or more within the previous 20") is looser and the first bullet is redundant.
- No UK-Spain IHT treaty. HMRC lists IHT conventions with Ireland, South Africa, USA, Netherlands, Sweden and
  Switzerland (modern), and older ones with France, Italy, India and Pakistan. Spain is not listed. Where there is
  no treaty, HMRC allows "Unilateral Relief" for similar foreign tax on assets located in that country.
- For a British person who moves to Spain: UK IHT scope on worldwide assets continues for the tail (3 to 10 tax
  years, by years of UK residence). Spanish residents are taxed on succession by obligación personal (Ley 29/1987
  art. 6) and get a credit for similar foreign tax (art. 23).

## 2. IRNR for UK residents and Modelo 210. Verdict: CORRECTED

Verified facts:
- TRLIRNR art. 25.1.a): "Con carácter general el 24 por 100. No obstante, el tipo de gravamen será el 19 por
  ciento cuando se trate de contribuyentes residentes en otro Estado miembro de la Unión Europea o del Espacio
  Económico Europeo con el que exista un efectivo intercambio de información tributaria". Art. 25.1.f): 19 % for
  dividends, interest and capital gains on transfers of assets (applies to every non-resident, UK included).
- Art. 24.6 (deductions): only EU/EEA residents (EEA with effective exchange of information) may deduct IRPF
  expenses linked to the Spanish income. Art. 24 is the tax base, art. 25 the rates.
- Brexit: the UK is no longer an EU Member State. AEAT: for income accrued from 1 Jan 2021 by UK residents the 19 %
  general rate "dejará de ser aplicable" and 24 % applies (rents, imputed income, employment income); art. 24.6
  expenses cannot be deducted; the art. 14 EU exemptions end; the art. 46 option to be taxed as an IRPF resident
  is not available; the main-home reinvestment relief for gains no longer applies. The UK-Spain treaty "continuará
  siendo aplicable" (for example, interest can still be exempt under the treaty).
- Rental income of a UK resident: 24 % on gross rent, no expenses (AEAT).
- Capital gains on Spanish property: 19 % for all non-residents (art. 25.1.f.3), not 24 %. The buyer withholds
  3 % of the price on account (art. 25.2). The seller files Modelo 210 within three months once one month has
  passed from the transfer (Orden EHA/3316/2010 art. 5.a, as restated by HAC/623/2026).
- Dividends and interest from Spain to a UK resident: internal rate 19 %. Treaty caps dividends at 10 % (art.
  10(2)(a)) and makes interest taxable only in the residence State (art. 11).
- Modelo 210, Orden HAC/623/2026 (BOE 23 June 2026, in force 24 June 2026), CORRECTS the brief:
  - It does not create the annual grouped return. AEAT: for rental income, from accruals of 2024 the grouping
    period "ha pasado de ser TRIMESTRAL a ANUAL".
  - It moves deadlines. Rents, grouped or separate, with an amount to pay: the first 20 days of April of the year
    after accrual (it was 1-20 January for grouped annual returns). Domiciliation 1-15 April. Imputed income of
    urban property: 1 April to 31 December of the following year (it was 1 January to 31 December); direct debit
    until 23 December.
  - Timing: grouped rents and imputed income apply from accruals of 2026 (first filing 1-20 April 2027). Separate
    rent returns: only for accruals from Q4 2026. Q2 and Q3 2026 keep the 20 July and 20 October deadlines. Imputed
    income of 2025 keeps 1 Jan to 31 Dec 2026.
  - New expenses breakdown annex and "days" and "ownership share" boxes, for returns filed from 1 Jan 2027.
  - Other income (dividends etc.) still files quarterly: 20 days of April, July, October, January. Zero-tax
    returns 1-20 January. Refunds from 1 February, four-year limit.
- Suggested sentence (inline link on the anchor): "From income accrued in 2026, [the Modelo 210 for rental
  income is due in the first 20 days of April of the following year](AEAT note URL)."

## 3. US-Spain treaty, 2013 Protocol, totalization agreement. Verdict: CORRECTED

Verified facts:
- Protocol signed at Madrid on 14 January 2013. BOE 23 October 2019 (BOE-A-2019-15166). In force 27 November 2019
  (three months after the last diplomatic note, Protocol art. XV). Effects: withholding taxes from that date; other
  taxes for tax years starting from it.
- What it changed (Spanish text opened):
  - Dividends (art. 10): 5 % if a company holds directly at least 10 % of the voting shares; 15 % otherwise
    (before: 10 % at 25 %, 15 %). Source-State tax is 0 % for a company holding 80 % or more for 12 months that
    meets the limitation-on-benefits tests, and for pension funds. Branch tax art. 14 abolished.
  - Interest (art. 11): taxable only in the State of residence (before: capped at 10 %). Exception: US contingent
    interest, capped at 10 % when the beneficial owner is Spanish.
  - Royalties (art. 12): taxable only in the State of residence (before 5 %, 8 % or 10 %).
  - Capital gains (art. 13): 13(4) replaced by gains on shares giving a right to enjoy immovable property
    (taxable where located); 13(6)-(7) merged into new 13(6): other gains taxable only in the transferor's State
    of residence. 13(2) (shares of entities mainly holding Spanish real estate) is untouched.
  - Pensions (art. 20): new 20(5) on income from a pension fund resident in the other State. The Memorandum of
    Understanding defines US "pension fund" to include 401(a)/401(k) plans, 403(b), IRAs (408) and Roth IRAs (408A).
  - Limitation of benefits (art. 17) and arbitration in the mutual agreement procedure (art. 26) rewritten.
  - Tie-breaker (art. 4(2)) is NOT changed: permanent home, then centre of vital interests, then habitual abode,
    then nationality, then mutual agreement.
  - Savings clause (art. 1(3)) is NOT changed: each State "puede someter a imposición a sus residentes ... y por
    razón de ciudadanía ... a sus ciudadanos, como si el Convenio no hubiese entrado en vigor". The Protocol only
    added art. 1(5) (GATS) and 1(6) (transparent entities).
- CORRECTION to the brief: social security benefits are art. 20(1)(b), not 20(2). Art. 20(1)(a): pensions for past
  employment "sólo pueden someterse a imposición" in the State of residence. Art. 20(1)(b): social security
  benefits paid by a State to a resident of the other State, or to a US citizen, "pueden someterse a imposición"
  in the paying State, which is not an exclusive right. Art. 20(2) covers annuities (residence only). Spain
  relieves double tax by credit (art. 24(1)); art. 24(3) treats US-citizen income taxable by the US as Spanish
  source "en la medida necesaria".
- Totalization agreement (SSA): signed at Madrid on 30 September 1986, entered into force 1 April 1988. Covers, for
  Spain, the General System (disability from ordinary illness or non-work injury, old age, death and survivorship)
  and the special systems (agricultural, maritime, coal miners, railroad, domestic employees, self-employed,
  commercial representatives, students, artists, authors, bullfighters, professional footballers). For the US:
  Title II of the Social Security Act (except sections 226, 226A, 228) and Chapters 2 and 21 of the Internal Revenue
  Code of 1954 (social security taxes). It covers social security contributions and benefits, not income tax.
  Employees are covered in the State of work (art. 4), with a five-year detached-worker rule (art. 5). Self-employed
  people are covered in the State where they reside (art. 4(2)), with a five-year transfer rule. SSA notes that
  workers exempted from Spanish coverage pay no Spanish contributions for sickness, health, unemployment, work
  accidents or family benefits.

## 4. Valencian wealth tax, state minimum, ITSGF. Verdict: CONFIRMED, with one correction

Verified facts:
- Ley 5/2026, de 31 de julio, art. 19 rewrites art. 8 of Ley 13/1997: "La base imponible de los sujetos pasivos
  por obligación personal del impuesto que residan habitualmente en la Comunitat Valenciana se reduce, en concepto
  de mínimo exento, en 2.000.000 euros." BOE 17 September 2026 (BOE-A-2026-19331); DOGV 10 August 2026; in force
  11 August 2026 (disposición final quinta: the day after DOGV publication).
- ATV: "en la Comunitat Valenciana el mínimo exento, es de 2.000.000 € con efectos desde el 11/08/2026, por lo
  que afecta al patrimonio del que sea titular a 31/12/2026 (que se declarará en el 2027), y sucesivos". The ATV
  table: 500 000 € up to 2024 (1 000 000 € for disability of 33 % psychic or 65 % physical/sensory), 1 000 000 € in
  2025, 2 000 000 € from 2026. Hisenda "Novedades tributarias 2026" lists the same change.
- CORRECTION to the plan (N-5, U-7): the Valencian minimum is only for people who are Spanish residents in the
  Comunitat (obligación personal). For non-residents, the state minimum applies: LIP art. 28 Tres, "El mínimo exento
  señalado en el apartado anterior [700.000 euros] será aplicable en el caso de sujetos pasivos no residentes que
  tributen por obligación personal ... y a los sujetos pasivos sometidos a obligación real". The AEAT 2025 manual
  says the same. A UK or US owner of a Valencian property who is not resident in Spain does not get 2 M€.
- State default minimum 700 000 € when the Community has none (LIP art. 28 Dos). Wealth tax accrues on 31 December.
  Non-residents are taxed on obligación real (LIP art. 5.1.b), on assets located in Spain.
- All non-residents, not only EU/EEA residents, may apply the regional rules of the Community where the highest
  value of their Spanish assets lies (LIP DA 4ª as amended by Ley 11/2021; AEAT 2025 manual).
- Supreme Court STS 1372/2025 (29 Oct 2025) and 1402/2025 (3 Nov 2025): the cuota-limit of art. 31 LIP applies to
  non-residents too. The 2025 Modelo 714 was adapted (Orden HAC/277/2026).
- ITSGF (Ley 38/2022 art. 3): applies to net wealth above 3 000 000 €; exempt minimum 700 000 € (also for obligación
  real, AEAT); complementary to wealth tax, not transferable to the Communities; same taxpayers as wealth tax
  (non-residents included); filing 1-31 July of the year after accrual (2025 returns: from 1 July 2026). The
  consolidated art. 3 says the tax is prorogued "en tanto no se produzca la revisión de la tributación patrimonial
  en el contexto de la reforma del sistema de financiación autonómica" (DA 5.2 RDL 8/2023). Orden HAC/652/2026
  (BOE 29 June 2026) adapts model 718 so that the cuota limit applies to obligación real too (TEAC, 18 Dec 2025).
- ATV also states the filing test: a return is due if the quota is payable, or, when the net base is above the
  minimum, if the gross value of assets exceeds 2 000 000 €.

## 5. Modelo 720 and 721. Verdict: CONFIRMED

Verified facts:
- Modelo 720 (RGAT arts. 42 bis, 42 ter, and 54 bis for real estate): three categories, accounts, securities and
  insurance, real estate. No duty to report a category when its value does not exceed 50 000 € (accounts: the
  31 December balances and the Q4 average balance, "conjuntamente"; if either limit is exceeded, report all
  accounts). Later years only if a category rises by more than 20 000 € against the last return, or on extinguished
  holdings. AEAT confirms 50 000 € for each of the three categories.
- Deadline: 1 January to 31 March of the following year (art. 42 bis.5, 42 ter.5, 42 quater.6). The 2025 return
  was due 1 Jan to 31 Mar 2026, plus four calendar days for technical failure (AEAT). The 2026 return follows the
  same rule: 1 Jan to 31 Mar 2027 (derived from the RGAT; the AEAT 2027 page is not published yet).
- Modelo 721 (virtual currencies abroad): art. 42 quater RGAT (added by RD 249/2023), Orden HFP/886/2023 (BOE-A-2023-
  17429, in force 30 July 2023). 50 000 € at 31 December, valued in euros at the December 31 price; filed 1 Jan to
  31 Mar; applies to coins held with custodians that safeguard private keys for third parties and that are not
  resident in Spain. AEAT FAQ: coins in wallets where you keep your private keys are not reportable.
- CJEU, Case C-788/19, 27 January 2022, ruled that Spain failed to fulfil its obligations under art. 63 TFEU and art.
  40 EEA Agreement by (1) taxing undeclared assets as "unjustified capital gains" with no possibility of
  limitation, (2) a proportional 150 % fine on top of flat fines, (3) flat fines that were disproportionate and
  not capped.
- Ley 5/2022 (BOE 10 March 2022, in force 11 March 2022): DF 4 rewrites DA 18ª LGT (reporting duty; adds virtual
  currencies); its sole repeal clause repeals DA 1ª and 2ª of Ley 7/2012 (the 5 000 € per item, 10 000 € minimum and
  150 % regime); DF 5 rewrites art. 39 LIRPF (unjustified gains now go into the period "respecto del que se
  descubran" unless the taxpayer proves earlier ownership before the limitation period).
- Penalties now: LGT art. 198: late or missing return with no loss to the Treasury is a minor offence, 20 € for each
  datum or set of data for one person or entity, minimum 300 €, maximum 20 000 €; half of that if filed late without
  a prior request. Art. 199 covers incomplete or inaccurate returns (200 € per datum for non-monetary data;
  up to 2 % of the amounts, minimum 500 €, for monetary data).
- Live EN post 27449 ("UK ISAs and Spanish Tax Residency", https://delaguialuzon.com/en/blog/uk-isas-spanish-tax-residency/,
  last updated 30 Sep 2026; matched by title, the post ID was not read) still says "€5,000-per-omission penalty
  regime": OUTDATED since 11 March 2022. Fix when that post is next edited.
- DGT V0890-24: rights in a UK pension plan are not reportable on Modelo 720 before a contingency under the plan
  occurs, unless the plan gives a life-insurance-type surrender right (art. 42 ter.3.a).
- Crypto: reuse `data/factcheck/8615.csv` (DAC8 collection from 1 Jan 2026, no Spanish transposition in BOE at
  2026-10-02; Modelo 721 penalties under LGT 198). Not re-opened today.

## 6. UK-Spain treaty: pensions, lump sum, ISAs. Verdict: CORRECTED (State Pension), UNVERIFIED (lump sum, ISAs)

Verified facts:
- The 2013 Convention (signed London 14 March 2013; BOE 15 May 2014) entered into force on 12 June 2014 (HMRC).
  HMRC publishes a synthesised text with the Multilateral Instrument (effective 2023); the MLI makes no change to
  arts. 17 and 18.
- Art. 17 (HMRC English): "Subject to the provisions of paragraph 2 of Article 18, pensions and other similar
  remuneration paid to an individual who is a resident of a Contracting State, shall be taxable only in that State."
  AEAT: a pension for past private-sector employment is taxed only in Spain for a Spanish resident.
- Art. 18(2): government service pensions, "paid by, or out of funds created by" a State for services rendered to it
  are taxable only in that State, except that they are taxable only in the other State if the individual is both
  resident and national there. AEAT: in Spain such a pension is exempt with progression (it counts for the rate on
  other income) unless the pensioner is a Spanish national, in which case only Spain taxes it. Art. 18(3) sends
  pensions for services in a State's economic activity back to art. 17.
- CORRECTION on the UK State Pension. DGT binding rulings V2460-21 (29 Sep 2021) and V1380-25 treated a UK state
  pension as a public pension under art. 18.2, "de acuerdo con los escasos datos aportados": paid out of funds created
  for services to the UK Government. Result: a British national resident in Spain is taxable only in the UK and the
  pension is exempt with progression in Spain; a Spanish national is taxable only in Spain. Do NOT write that the
  State Pension falls under art. 17 and is taxed only in Spain. See open question 3.
- Treaty articles that matter for UK investors: art. 10 dividends (source State cap 10 %, 15 % for qualifying REIT-type
  income); art. 11 interest (residence State only); art. 13(6) gains on other assets (residence State only); art. 13(1)
  gains on real estate (taxable where located); art. 22 relief by credit in Spain; art. 4(2) tie-breaker (permanent
  home, vital interests, habitual abode, nationality, mutual agreement).
- ISAs: the Convention text (BOE Spanish text and HMRC PDF) contains no provision on ISAs. Covered UK taxes are
  income tax, corporation tax and capital gains tax (art. 2(3)). So no treaty clause protects the ISA wrapper.
- UK pension plan rights are not reportable on Modelo 720 before the plan pays benefits (DGT V0890-24, above).

## Method notes

- BOE consolidated text read through the BOE open-data API (`/datosabiertos/api/legislacion-consolidada/id/<id>/texto`),
  which returns the in-force version of each article, plus `diario_boe/txt.php` pages for original texts.
- DGT rulings read in the DGT database (petete) through a browser session, because WebFetch only gets the search shell.
- SSA pages block automated fetching (HTTP 403); they were read in a browser session. EUR-Lex also needs a browser.
- IRS and Treasury treaty PDFs are scanned images; the BOE texts replace them.
- AISA `fact_check` was not used.
